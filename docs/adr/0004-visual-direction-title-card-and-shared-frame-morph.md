# Visual direction: title-card layout with expandable nav sidebar, shared-frame-morph transitions

The site's visual language (issue #11) needed a base palette/layout and a step-transition style before any scaffolding code could be written. Both were prototyped rather than decided from description — the base-palette question in particular needed a second round after the first was rejected outright.

**Palette/layout**: the first round of 5 palette prototypes (`prototyp/palette.html`) was rejected as generic for three reasons: all 5 variants shared one layout and only swapped color/font (a tweak, not a real option set), each individual look was itself an overused "AI mood board" cliché (dark terminal, parchment manuscript, blueprint grid, stark mono) regardless of layout, and the whole set was too safe to have a point of view. We re-ran design research via the lazyweb MCP with queries aimed at structurally distinctive real sites instead of more SaaS-onboarding-shaped references, producing a second round of 4 genuinely different compositions (`prototyp/palette-round2.html`). From there we settled on combining that round's title-card content treatment (a large ghost numeral overlapping the step heading, from an independent designer's portfolio reference) with its sidebar-as-chrome idea (from a bold color-blocked portfolio reference) — but upgraded the sidebar from a static dot rail to an expand/collapse control whose expanded state shows the site's real navigation (all 4 named sections, current section's steps nested and highlighted), not just a progress readout. We considered keeping the sidebar as a fixed, always-visible progress-only rail (simpler, no interaction state) but rejected it: with 4 sections and the Intro to AI section's two live-delivery parts, a self-paced reader needs to actually navigate the site from the indicator, not just read their position in it. The sidebar's color/font were then corrected to match the body exactly (light paper background, dark ink, same type) rather than the dark-chrome treatment inherited from its source reference — a follow-up fix once the mismatch was pointed out.

**Step transitions**: five transition primitives were prototyped (`prototyp/transitions.html`): universal slide, universal crossfade, a coupled diagram-morph/screenshot-crossfade split, universal zoom, and a shared-frame-morph where the step's visual frame changes shape/scale on every transition. The shared-frame-morph won on the frame idea but its crossfaded inner content was rejected as feeling disconnected from the frame's motion. A second round (`prototyp/transitions-round2.html`) tried four alternative content behaviors — slide-with-frame, clip-path wipe, scale-with-frame, and a hard cut — and settled on an explicit merge of the last two: the frame and its content now scale together on the exact same ratio and timing, with no opacity fade at all, so the transition reads as one continuous physical zoom rather than two independently-timed animations. We considered keeping per-content-type coupling (diagrams morph, screenshots crossfade, as explored in round 1's "coupled" variant) as the universal rule, but decided that's better left as an optional per-diagram refinement layered on top of the universal frame+content rule, not a replacement for it — most of the site's step content is screenshot evidence, not diagrams, so the universal rule needs to work well on its own.

Both decisions are written up as the actual design brief at `docs/design-brief.md`, checked explicitly against root `CLAUDE.md`'s avoid-list. The full prototyping process — including the rejected first round and why — is kept as teaching material at `prototyp/README.md`, since this site documents its own AI-assisted build.
